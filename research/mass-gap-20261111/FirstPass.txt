uniformly over the characters i, with M_w depending on the word.

WHY THIS ESTIMATE DOES NOT YET GIVE A GAP

Take the finite measure dmu(E) = E exp(-E) dE on (0,infinity). Directly,

    mu((0,u]) = 1 - (1+u) exp(-u),
    0 < mu((0,u]) <= integral_0^u E dE = u^2/2  for every u > 0,
    integral_0^infinity exp(-tE) dmu(E) = 1/(1+t)^2.

The upper bound follows from exp(-E) <= 1; strict positivity follows from
strict positivity of the density on every interval inside (0,u).
Thus precisely quadratic low-energy control and inverse-square heat decay
coexist with spectral weight arbitrarily close to zero.

For an operator realization let K = C direct-sum L2((0,infinity),mu), with
Omega = (1,0), and H(c,f) = (0,E f(E)) on the multiplication-operator domain.
H is nonnegative and self-adjoint. Its kernel is exactly C Omega, since E>0
mu-almost everywhere. Every interval (0,u) has a nonzero spectral projection;
there is no positive gap. The centered unit vector (0,1) has heat pairing
1/(1+t)^2. Bounded compactly supported functions are dense in the L2 part,
and satisfy the same type of quadratic/heat estimate with vector-dependent
constants. This also addresses the fact that the repository constants depend
on the word rather than only on its Hilbert norm.

This is a counterexample to the proposed SPECTRAL INFERENCE, not to the full
Yang–Mills construction. It is not a four-dimensional Wightman theory with
the repository's Wilson, OPE, or trace properties. It does not establish
logical independence of mass gap from all those stronger properties.

check_obstruction.py, using SymPy 1.14.0, exactly checked the two integrals,
the total mass 1, and exp(mt)/(1+t)^2 -> infinity for symbolic m>0.
These are algebraic checks, not formal verification of the operator model.

A PRECISE SUFFICIENT LEMMA, WITH PROOF

Let H>=0 be a self-adjoint Hamiltonian, with spectral PVM P, and normalized
vacuum Omega satisfying ker H = C Omega. Let D be a dense subset of
Omega-perpendicular. Suppose that there exists ONE m>0 such that for each
psi in D there is a finite C_psi>=0 with

    0 <= <psi, exp(-tH) psi> <= C_psi exp(-m t),       t>=1.       (*)

Then P((0,m))=0, hence spec(H) is contained in {0} union [m,infinity).
The constants C_psi need not be uniform over D. The rate m MUST be uniform.

Proof. Set mu_psi(B)=<psi,P(B)psi>. For 0<a<m and t>=1, positivity yields

    exp(-a t) mu_psi((0,a])
      <= integral exp(-tE) dmu_psi(E)
      <= C_psi exp(-m t).

Thus mu_psi((0,a]) <= C_psi exp(-(m-a)t). Taking t to infinity gives zero.
Taking an increasing countable sequence a approaching m gives
mu_psi((0,m))=0. Since P((0,m)) is an orthogonal projection,
||P((0,m))psi||^2 = mu_psi((0,m))=0. Boundedness and density imply that it
annihilates all of Omega-perpendicular. It also annihilates Omega, whose
spectral measure is concentrated at zero. Therefore P((0,m))=0.
The spectral theorem gives the stated exclusion of the open interval. End.

This is a standard spectral reduction, not a new Yang–Mills theorem.
For the repository, use its spectral measure and heat operator, center the
cyclic word vectors, and establish the required density for the particular
finite-word class. The public file MassGapTarget.lean expresses the gap using
the existing joint energy-momentum PVM, avoiding an extra Hamiltonian object.
The target asks for one common m per chosen Wilson family across all volume
refinements, which is stronger than permitting a separate gap in each limit.

THE ACTUAL REMAINING YANG–MILLS PROBLEM

Prove (*) for the SAME selected hierarchy as the construction, with a positive
rate that survives both cutoff removal and infinite volume. Short-distance
OPE asymptotics, exclusion of the specified free-field profiles, finite-volume
positivity, and the displayed polynomial estimate do not supply that proof.

A useful concrete intermediate target is a strict contraction at a fixed
physical Euclidean time tau>0 on the centered subspace:

    ||T(tau)|_(Omega-perpendicular)|| <= q < 1.

For a positive self-adjoint heat semigroup this would give
m >= -log(q)/tau. It is a reformulation of the gap at continuum level, not an
independent argument. Its potential use is to derive q from quantitative
finite-cutoff Yang–Mills estimates and pass the bound through the construction.
One must control the vacuum subtraction, operator/state identification, and
both limits; a finite-lattice gap whose q approaches 1 is insufficient.
A proof just for the stress-trace two-point function does not cover the whole
Hilbert space without a separate density/cyclicity argument for that channel.

Do not transfer an averaged unselected correlation estimate to a selected
phase without proof. Centering can differ by phase. Uniform all-phase heat
bounds, or an appropriate positive spectral disintegration plus continuity
argument, would resolve this additional issue; raw averaged cancellation does
not. The repository's full-support/continuity machinery is a potential aid,
not an already supplied exponential estimate.
